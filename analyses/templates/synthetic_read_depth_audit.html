1. What did we actually test?

We analyzed ${run_count} completed synthetic runs: 57 within-depth fits and 20 mixed-depth fits. The main matched-depth comparison uses the 27 centered cumulative models: three depths, each with nine panel sizes \(N=2,\ldots,10\). We did not train, infer new profiles, change reference weights, or fit a new statistical model. “Tested” here means evaluated these post-hoc comparisons, not performed a preregistered hypothesis test or established a causal effect over independent training runs.

3. From programmed kinetics to observations and model predictions

Define the full-sense-CDS normalization operator:

\[ \mathcal M_t[\mathbf x]_i=\frac{x_i}{\ell_t^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_t}x_j}, \qquad K_{ti}=\mathcal M_t[\boldsymbol\tau_t]_i, \qquad q_{tri}=\mathcal M_t[\mathbf O_{tr}]_i. \]

The supplied generation protocol and count metadata specify:

\[ \begin{aligned} m_{dtri}^{(C)} &= Cq_{tri}b_{dti}, & Y_{dtri}^{(C)} &\sim\operatorname{NB2}(m_{dtri}^{(C)},\alpha_{\rm sim}),\\ \operatorname{Var}(Y_{dtri}^{(C)}\mid q,b) &=m_{dtri}^{(C)}+\alpha_{\rm sim}(m_{dtri}^{(C)})^2, &\alpha_{\rm sim}&=0.1,\\ \bar Y_{dti}^{(C)} &=\frac{Y_{dt1i}^{(C)}+Y_{dt2i}^{(C)}}2, & \mathbb E[\bar Y_{dti}^{(C)}\mid q,b]&=C\bar q_{ti}b_{dti}. \end{aligned} \]

For conditionally independent replica count draws, their mean has conditional variance \(\frac14\sum_{r=1}^2[m_{dtri}^{(C)}+\alpha_{\rm sim}(m_{dtri}^{(C)})^2]\). The stored integer-rounded “mean” row is neither this exact arithmetic mean nor a third independent replica.

The model's sequence-only shared output and mass-free count prediction have the forms:

\[ \widetilde{\mathbf L}_t^{(C,N)}=f_{\widehat\theta_{C,N}}(\mathbf s_t), \qquad S_{dt}^{(C)}=\frac1{|\mathcal J_t|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal J_t}\bar Y_{dti}^{(C)}, \qquad \widehat\mu_{dti}^{(C,N)} =S_{dt}^{(C)}\widehat\gamma_{dti}^{(C,N)}\widetilde L_{ti}^{(C,N)}. \]

The terminal target appended in preprocessing is zero. It contributes to the model-valid mean but is removed for the sense-CDS comparisons. The shared branch does not take the validation count vector as input; counts affect its trained parameters, while \(S_{dt}\) scales the count prediction separately.

The noise argument is correct: for a single NB2 draw, relative conditional variance is

\[ \frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid q,b)}{\mathbb E[Y\mid q,b]^2}=\frac1m+\alpha_{\rm sim}. \]

At nominal \(q_{tri}b_{dti}=1\), this is \(4.1,\ 0.6,\ 0.15\) for depths \(0.25,2,20\). These are relative variances of individual draws, not prediction errors of the trained sequence model.

4. Why introduce the proxy \(\mathbf H_t^{(N)}\)?

For the centered within-depth models, \(N\ge2\), the saved reference convention is uniform:

\[ \pi_d^{(N)}=\frac1N,\qquad \sum_{d=1}^N\pi_d^{(N)}=1,\qquad \sum_{d=1}^N\pi_d^{(N)}\log\widehat\gamma_{dti}^{(C,N)}=0. \]

The model also centers each correction over its model-valid positions: \(|\mathcal J_t|^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal J_t}\log\widehat\gamma_{dti}=0\). The pointwise reference constraint implies the exact identity for the predicted count profiles:

\[ \prod_{d=1}^N(\widehat\mu_{dti})^{\pi_d} =\widetilde L_{ti}\prod_{d=1}^N S_{dt}^{\pi_d}. \]

If the predicted means could exactly match the noiseless consensus means \(C_d\bar q_{ti}b_{dti}\), their weighted geometric mean would have positional shape \(\bar q_{ti}G_{ti}^{(N)}\), where:

\[ G_{ti}^{(N)}=\exp\!\left(\sum_{d=1}^N\pi_d^{(N)}\log b_{dti}\right) =\prod_{d=1}^N b_{dti}^{1/N},\qquad H_{ti}^{(N)}=\mathcal M_t[\mathbf K_t\odot\mathbf G_t^{(N)}]_i. \]

Here \(\odot\) denotes elementwise multiplication. We substituted \(\mathbf K_t\) for \(\overline{\mathbf q}_t\) to construct a diagnostic; we did not measure an exact unnoised traffic target. For fixed \(N\), \(\mathbf H_t^{(N)}\) is identical across depths on a given transcript because \(\mathbf K_t\), the injected multipliers and the reference weights are unchanged. It changes with \(N\).

What the proxy is not: it is not \(\mathbf K_t\), not a count observation, not an exact infinite-depth prediction, and not an absolute recovery ceiling. The mass-free target scale and the extra positional gamma constraint can prevent an exact simultaneous fit of all generating means. Consequently, RMSE against \(\mathbf H_t\) must not be called a pure sampling-variance estimate or silently relabeled “biological recovery.” No known kinetic or bias target was used to set weights or train these models.

For illustration, a site affected only by a multiplier 5.5 contributes \(5.5^{1/2}\approx2.35\) to \(G_{ti}^{(2)}\), but \(5.5^{1/10}\approx1.19\) to \(G_{ti}^{(10)}\). More heterogeneous datasets can dilute a common reference distortion; more reads alone do not remove it.

5. Exactly how the metrics and cohorts were constructed

5.1 Alignment and scale

Each saved profile has exactly one more model-valid position than its kinetic reference. We retain \(\widehat L_{ti}^{(C,N)}=\widetilde L_{ti}^{(C,N)}\) for \(i=1,\ldots,\ell_t\), then score only \(\mathcal I_t=\{11,\ldots,\ell_t-10\}\). We do not silently truncate mismatched profiles, smooth, filter observed-zero positions, or pool codons across transcripts.

\[ \frac1{\ell_t+1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal J_t}\widetilde L_{ti}=1 \quad\text{does not imply}\quad \frac1{\ell_t}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell_t}\widehat L_{ti}=1. \]

Primary RMSE preserves the saved scale after removal of the terminal position. The separately exported rmse_K_sense_mean1 is a normalization sensitivity check, not the plotted definition. \(\mathbf H_t\) is normalized over all sense codons before trimming, never over the trimmed interior.

5.2 Transcript-level and cohort-level statistics

For two aligned profiles \(\mathbf x_t,\mathbf y_t\), let \(\bar x_{t,\mathcal I}=m_t^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_t}x_{ti}\), and similarly for \(\bar y_{t,\mathcal I}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} r_t(\mathbf x_t,\mathbf y_t)&= \frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_t}(x_{ti}-\bar x_{t,\mathcal I})(y_{ti}-\bar y_{t,\mathcal I})} {\sqrt{\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_t}(x_{ti}-\bar x_{t,\mathcal I})^2 \sum_{i\in\mathcal I_t}(y_{ti}-\bar y_{t,\mathcal I})^2}},\\ D_t(\mathbf x_t,\mathbf y_t)&=\frac1{m_t}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_t}(x_{ti}-y_{ti})^2, \qquad E_t(\mathbf x_t,\mathbf y_t)=\sqrt{D_t(\mathbf x_t,\mathbf y_t)},\\ \bar r_{\mathcal V}^{(C,N)}(A)&=\frac1{|\mathcal V|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal V} r_t(\widehat{\mathbf L}_t^{(C,N)},\mathbf A_t^{(N)}),\\ \bar E_{\mathcal V}^{(C,N)}(A)&=\frac1{|\mathcal V|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal V} E_t(\widehat{\mathbf L}_t^{(C,N)},\mathbf A_t^{(N)}),\qquad A\in\{K,H\}. \end{aligned} \]

For \(A=K\), the superscript \((N)\) on the reference is omitted: kinetics do not change with panel size. All cohort means give each transcript weight one. In particular,

\[ \frac1{|\mathcal V|}\sum_t\sqrt{D_t} \ne\sqrt{\frac1{|\mathcal V|}\sum_tD_t} \quad\text{in general}. \]

For the count-prediction control, datasets are averaged within a transcript before transcripts are averaged:

\[ r_{t,\rm count}^{(C,N)}=\frac1N\sum_{d=1}^N r_t(\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_{dt}^{(C,N)},\overline{\mathbf Y}_{dt}^{(C)}),\qquad \bar r_{\mathcal V,\rm count}^{(C,N)}=\frac1{|\mathcal V|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal V}r_{t,\rm count}^{(C,N)}. \]

This control requires a defined PCC for every included dataset. A transcript with fewer than two scored positions, nonfinite values, or a centered norm-product no greater than \(10^{-12}\) is not assigned PCC zero.

7. What the uncertainty intervals mean

For a comparison cohort \(\mathcal V\) of size \(n\), each bootstrap draw samples transcripts with replacement:

\[ t_1^{*(b)},\ldots,t_n^{*(b)}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\operatorname{Uniform}(\mathcal V), \qquad b=1,\ldots,B. \]

The same sampled IDs are reused across all depths, panel sizes and metrics within that cohort. The two cohorts have their own draws. For example:

\[ R_N^{*(b)}= \frac{n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n E_{t_j^{*(b)}}(\widehat{\mathbf L}_{t_j^{*(b)}}^{(0.25,N)},\mathbf H_{t_j^{*(b)}}^{(N)})} {n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n E_{t_j^{*(b)}}(\widehat{\mathbf L}_{t_j^{*(b)}}^{(20,N)},\mathbf H_{t_j^{*(b)}}^{(N)})}, \quad \operatorname{CI}_{95\%}(R_N)= \left[Q_{0.025}(R_N^*),Q_{0.975}(R_N^*)\right]. \]

The new figure uses \(B=${figure_bootstrap}\), bootstrap seed ${bootstrap_seed}. The earlier audit's paired difference table below used \(B=3{,}000\), seed 42. Its sign convention is high minus low:

\[ \Delta_{N,E(H)}=\bar E_{\mathcal V_{\rm LH}}^{(20,N)}(H) -\bar E_{\mathcal V_{\rm LH}}^{(0.25,N)}(H), \quad \Delta_{N,E(H)}<0\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{less proxy error at high depth}. \]
${contrast_table}
These are pointwise percentile intervals, not simultaneous bands or multiplicity-adjusted hypothesis tests. They account for transcript sampling conditional on the saved models, not variability over training seeds, count-sampling seeds or independent simulator trajectories. All training seeds are 42. Matching validation IDs does not make the historical training sets or the original \(w_{dt}^{(C)}\) values identical across depths. We have not demonstrated a causal depth effect or statistical equivalence.

8. Why can proxy error deteriorate while kinetic recovery barely changes?

Define two per-codon deviations on the same scored interior:

\[ a_{ti}^{(N)}=H_{ti}^{(N)}-K_{ti},\qquad e_{ti}^{(C,N)}=\widehat L_{ti}^{(C,N)}-H_{ti}^{(N)},\qquad \widehat L_{ti}^{(C,N)}-K_{ti}=a_{ti}^{(N)}+e_{ti}^{(C,N)}. \]

Squaring gives an exact identity, with no assumption of independent or orthogonal errors:

\[ \begin{aligned} D_t(\widehat{\mathbf L}_t,\mathbf K_t) &=D_t(\mathbf H_t,\mathbf K_t)+D_t(\widehat{\mathbf L}_t,\mathbf H_t)+\chi_t,\\ \chi_t&=\frac2{m_t}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_t}(H_{ti}-K_{ti})(\widehat L_{ti}-H_{ti}). \end{aligned} \]

Negative \(\chi_t\) means partial cancellation; positive \(\chi_t\) means amplification. The table reports means of these per-transcript MSEs on the same ${paired_n} transcripts, not squares of mean RMSEs.

${decomposition_table}

At \(N=10\), low-depth deviations from \(\mathbf H_t\) partially cancel its difference from \(\mathbf K_t\); the high-depth cross term is positive. Thus a model closer to the diagnostic proxy can still be farther from kinetics. This identity explains the arithmetic of the observed reversal, not why optimization selected those deviations, and it is not a biological-versus-technical variance decomposition.

9. What changes under equal versus depth-ranked reference weights?

For the mixed-depth construction, use \(f=1,\ldots,F\) for bias family and \(h=1,2,3\) for depth \(0.25,2,20\). Dataset \(d=(f,h)\), so \(N=3F\), and \(b_{(f,h),ti}=b_{fti}\). The weights are:

\[ \pi_{f,h}^{\rm equal}=\frac1{3F},\qquad \pi_{f,h}^{\rm rank}=\frac{a_h}{6F},\qquad(a_1,a_2,a_3)=(1,2,3),\qquad \sum_{h=1}^3\pi_{f,h}^{\rm equal}=\sum_{h=1}^3\pi_{f,h}^{\rm rank}=\frac1F. \]

Both complete reference distributions sum to one, and their total mass per bias family is identical. Therefore:

\[ \log G_{ti}^{\rm equal}=\log G_{ti}^{\rm rank} =\frac1F\sum_{f=1}^F\log b_{fti},\qquad \mathbf H_t^{\rm equal}=\mathbf H_t^{\rm rank}. \]

The same is true of the known reference-centered correction target under this balanced construction. To see this, define the two-way centering operator, using \(b_{dt,\ell_t+1}=1\) at the appended boundary:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal C_\pi[z]_{dti} &=z_{dti}-\sum_e\pi_ez_{eti}-\langle z_{dt}\rangle_{\mathcal J_t} +\sum_e\pi_e\langle z_{et}\rangle_{\mathcal J_t},\\ b_{dti}^{\rm ref,\pi}&=\exp\!\left(\mathcal C_\pi[\log b]_{dti}\right), \qquad\langle z_{dt}\rangle_{\mathcal J_t}=\frac1{|\mathcal J_t|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_t}z_{dtj}. \end{aligned} \]

Because the weighted sums over repeated bias families are unchanged, \(b^{\rm ref,equal}=b^{\rm ref,rank}\) here as well. This corrects the previous report's overly general statement that the two conventions necessarily use different known gamma targets. They can differ in general, but not for these balanced repeated multipliers. No new gamma-recovery scores were calculated in this audit.

The learned raw gamma profiles need not be identical across depths: they are trained on different noisy observations. Weighting their learned scores differently can still affect centering, gradients and fitted outputs. Equality of the known target factor does not assert equality of trained networks.

What would strengthen the depth claim?

Use common training/validation/test identities across depths, repeat training and count-sampling seeds, and cross depth with the number of training transcripts. Export the unnoised \(\mathbf q_{tr}\) and generating means so that recovery of \(\mathbf K_t\), \(\overline{\mathbf q}_t\), and \(\mathcal M_t[\overline{\mathbf q}_t\odot\mathbf G_t]\) can be distinguished. Keep these as evaluation targets, not sources of reference weights. None of those new experiments was run here.
