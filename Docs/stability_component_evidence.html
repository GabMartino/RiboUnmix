What do Lbio, gamma and ranking actually contribute?

This report explains why a model trained with model.mean_correction=unity can produce reproducible shared profiles, what the latest partial results establish, and which experiments would distinguish useful dataset corrections from a convenient decomposition.

1. The high PCC measures reproducibility between models

The main stability plots compare Lbio from one fitted model with Lbio from another on the same held-out sequences. They do not compare the predicted observation mean μ with measured ribosome counts y.

Shared-profile reproducibility

PCC(Lbiopanel A, Lbiopanel B) asks whether different dataset collections produce similar profiles. It can be high even when both models miss dataset-specific effects or share a common bias.

Observed-profile fit

PCC(μdt, ydt) asks how well the predicted profile matches a dataset's observations. The available values are logged on validation transcripts at the selected checkpoint, so they are selection-dependent diagnostics.

What still works when gamma is fixed to one?

μdti = Sdt · Lbio,ti   when   γdti = 1
  • The sequence model is still trained. It can learn a compromise profile supported by shared sequence-dependent patterns across datasets.
  • Datasets still have different depth scales Sdt. These scales come from the targets. They explain scale accommodation, but cannot explain improved PCC because PCC is invariant to a positive scalar multiplier.
  • Local reliability weights still act. Less reliable dataset–transcript observations can receive less loss weight even in the equal-reference or gamma=1 arms.
  • Dispersion remains learned. Alpha changes the negative-binomial variance and training gradients. It can accommodate residual variability; it does not restore a dataset-dependent mean-shape correction.
  • Common biases can also be reproducible. A sequence predictor fitted to different panels can repeat a protocol effect present across those panels. Agreement does not distinguish that effect from shared biology.

Directly reading the four shared-only raw test exports confirms gamma is exactly one at every valid position. This is not an accidentally active correction branch. Their targets are all ones because this prediction pass supplies a dummy interface row for sequence-only inference.

The shared-only baseline cannot give the same transcript different normalized mean shapes for different datasets. Its good cross-model PCC is compatible with that limitation. No observed-count PCC or RMSE should be computed from the dummy test targets.

Implementation: unity mean correction and target scale · sequence-only prediction rows · the actual model-to-model comparisons.

Why RMSE deserves a separate check

For two mean-one profiles with standard deviations σx, σy and PCC r, the identity is:

RMSE² = (σx − σy)² + 2σxσy(1 − r)

The general expression also includes the squared difference of profile means; that term is negligible here. I checked this identity against the saved transcript-level metrics: the largest residual is below 10−12. Thus similar PCC can coexist with different RMSE, and reducing profile amplitude can lower RMSE without improving biological accuracy.

Two limits of the gamma ablation

  • Dispersion context changes: alpha's head receives detached context features. When gamma=1 removes mean-branch gradients, that context encoder no longer learns. The existing comparison is a whole-system ablation. Fresh fits with the same fixed alpha in both arms would isolate gamma under matched dispersion.
  • Observation calibration is incomplete: mass_conservation=false. The full-equal validation mean-ratio diagnostic is about 1.32–1.74 across panels, whereas the gamma=1 diagnostic is 1 by construction. Better PCC and NLL do not imply exact count-scale calibration.

3. Ranking has an effect, but the evidence does not support a universal gain

Quality ranking changes the weights π used to center gamma. It changes the reference against which the model separates shared and dataset-specific structure. It does not directly multiply the observation losses by QC rank.

This is preliminary support for stabilization within the tested prefixes, not evidence for monotonic protection against all lower-ranked data. The 80- and 114-dataset endpoints are not yet available.

Across panels, linear ranking: the mean PCC advantage over equal is only +0.0027, with gains in 3/6 pairs. Mean RMSE is worse by 0.0477, with improvement in only 1/6 pairs. The mean PCC advantage over reversed linear weights is +0.0335, but the RMSE contrast again favors reversed weights in 5/6 pairs. Direction and amplitude effects therefore need separate interpretation.

Cubic ranking: on the same three completed panel pairs, mean PCC is 0.6980 versus 0.8050 for equal, and RMSE is 0.6333 versus 0.3634. All three contrasts worsen. In the cumulative experiment it improves adjacent PCC and RMSE over equal only for the first of four available additions. Stronger concentration has not produced stronger stabilization in this snapshot.

A defensible claim is that the reference policy changes the learned decomposition, with context-dependent effects on reproducibility. The present results do not justify “quality ranking consistently improves L_bio,” “cubic ranking is better,” or “a stronger QC reference necessarily gets closer to biology.”

There is also a useful diagnostic pattern: full-model validation PCC and replica NLL remain similar across policies even when the shared-profile agreement changes substantially. That is consistent with alternative decompositions giving similar observation fits; it is not yet proof that gamma compensates for every change in L_bio. A same-dataset, same-transcript comparison of L_bio, log-gamma and their product is needed to test that mechanism directly.

Use “shared sequence-derived profile under a specified reference” for Lbio and “dataset-conditioned relative deviations” for gamma until external evidence supports a stronger biological/technical interpretation. The constraints set the scale and reference convention. They cannot determine whether a pattern shared across all datasets is biological or a shared technical artifact.

General methodological context: Locatello et al., ICML 2019 motivates explicit assumptions and validation for disentangled representations; its unsupervised impossibility result is not being asserted as a theorem about this dataset-conditioned model. The distinction between removing batch effects and preserving biology is also central to Luecken et al., Nature Methods 2022; this is a validation analogy, not ribosome-profiling evidence.

5. First priority: test whether gamma is useful for the correct dataset

This is a more direct functional test than a cluster plot. It can use the existing checkpoint, so it requires additional inference rather than new training.

  1. Freeze the selected full checkpoint. Use genuine held-out transcript observations for known training datasets, with the saved folds and reliability references. Keep all model parameters fixed.
  2. Predict three means for each observed (d, t) pair: the learned gamma for d; gamma=1; and gamma from another selected dataset σ(d), evaluated on the same transcript t.
  3. Hold Lbio, the target dataset's alpha, its depth scale S and evaluation weights fixed. Swap only gamma. Changing the dataset input to the whole forward pass would also change alpha and would confound this test.
  4. Compare held-out PCC and replicate NLL. Report paired changes per transcript and per dataset, plus macro summaries that do not let the largest source family dominate. Distinguish profile fit conditional on observed depth from predicting absolute abundance.
  5. Use several prespecified dataset derangements (permutations with no dataset assigned to itself) and stratified swaps as sensitivity checks. These are negative controls, not additional training seeds. Report uncertainty that respects repeated transcripts and related datasets.
Correct: μdti = Sdt Lbio,ti γdti
Unit: μdti0 = Sdt Lbio,ti
Swapped: μdtiswap = Sdt Lbio,ti γσ(d),ti

If correct gamma consistently beats both controls, it supports useful dataset-specific correction on unseen transcripts. It still does not prove that every correction is technical: real experimental-condition differences can also be dataset-specific.

Setting gamma=1 after fitting the full model tests reliance on its trained correction. Freshly training a gamma=1 model tests whether the rest of the system can adapt without that branch. Both comparisons are useful, but their answers are different and should be reported separately.

6. Gamma clustering: make it a reproducible fingerprint analysis

First obtain comparable predictions

The current sequence-only exports have one requested dataset identity per transcript, not the entire reference panel. In the four full-equal exports these identities are weber_2024, calviello_2016, patel_2020 and woo_2018 respectively. The compact profile files contain Lbio only. These artifacts cannot supply a clustering of all 114 dataset gamma profiles.

Evaluate a common transcript × dataset grid within one fitted model, using only dataset identities whose embeddings were trained in that model. The N=40 full checkpoint can support a pilot now; use N=114 once available for the complete atlas. A 29-dataset panel checkpoint does not provide trained corrections for the other 85 datasets merely because their encoding IDs exist.

Use the same held-out sequence panel for every dataset, with original codon coordinates, deterministic evaluation and no observed-count input to the fingerprint construction. Stream sequence batches and aggregate distances or feature summaries so the entire grid need not stay in memory. Keep observation-based inference for the functional test separate.

Use log-gamma and preserve a common reference

Let gdti = log γdti. A distance that gives every probe transcript equal weight is:

D²(d,e) = (1 / |T|) ∑t ∈ T [ (1 / length(t)) ∑i (gdti − geti)² ]

This preserves correction magnitude while avoiding domination by the exponential scale of raw gamma. It also avoids letting long transcripts carry more weight just because they contain more codons. Use the common sequence grid rather than a different observed-transcript intersection for each dataset pair.

There is a useful algebraic property. With u the raw log score and adt its CDS mean, the configured centering gives:

gdti − geti = (udti − adt) − (ueti − aet)

The shared reference-center terms cancel. Thus changing only π in a frozen model leaves these within-model pairwise log-gamma distances unchanged, provided the dataset set, raw scores and scale convention stay fixed. Comparing distances across separately retrained policies tests a training effect, rather than an automatic translation caused by recentering. Naive correlations between individually centered gamma profiles do not have this invariance.

Do not directly combine raw log-gamma vectors from four separately fitted panels into one 114-dataset clustering: their learned decompositions and reference centers differ. Start with one common model. For nested N comparisons, restrict distances to datasets present in both models.

What to visualize and validate

  1. Show a dataset-distance heatmap and a dendrogram. A PCA or MDS view is supplementary. A two-dimensional embedding alone is not evidence that meaningful clusters exist.
  2. Build interpretable fingerprints. Summarize log-gamma by relative CDS position, distance from start/stop, and codon/context, on the same probe sequences. Show uncertainty and feature support; separate magnitude from shape if reporting a standardized sensitivity view.
  3. Test transcript reproducibility. Split probe transcripts into two disjoint sets, build two distance matrices, and assess neighbor/rank-distance agreement. Bootstrap transcripts, or genes when multiple isoforms occur, to assess cluster stability. This does not estimate training-seed variability.
  4. Validate against external annotations. Use protocol, nuclease, inhibitor treatment, laboratory/source or other documented metadata. Report within-label versus between-label distances, nearest-neighbor retrieval and label-permutation controls. Many source families have only one dataset, so do not force one cluster per source or treat all dataset pairs as independent replicates.
  5. Compare reference policies on the same grid. Ask whether neighboring datasets and interpretable effects persist under equal, ranked and reversed fits. An association with the QC rank used to define π is descriptive and is not independent confirmation that ranking improved biology.
  6. Check the negative control. Gamma=1 gives zero log-gamma distances. A clustering algorithm can still assign arbitrary labels to those identical points; such labels must not be interpreted.

A successful result would support reproducible dataset-conditioned correction fingerprints that associate with documented experimental factors. Their interpretation as purely technical nuisance requires separating protocol from biological condition. Similarity of learned dataset-ID embeddings alone would not establish that.

Diagnostics to include before interpreting gamma or alpha

  • Inspect gamma tails and bound activation. In the four full-equal single-identity probe exports, the 99.9th percentiles are roughly 4.95–8.20, but rare maxima reach about 8.6 × 103–3.6 × 104. These are diagnostics of four requested dataset identities, not a distribution over all 114 datasets or observed-count errors. The configured raw-log bound is applied before centering, so it is not a bound on final gamma.
  • Report the effective alpha used by the loss and the fraction at its clipping bounds. The current nb_alpha_mean diagnostic averages the unbounded head output, while the loss clamps log-alpha; the logged mean therefore need not equal the effective likelihood dispersion.
  • For identical (d, t, i) across separately fitted policies, compare changes in log Lbio, log-gamma and their sum. Opposing changes with a stable sum would provide direct evidence of decomposition sensitivity; current shared-profile agreement alone cannot show this.

Source functions: fixed-reference centering · raw bound and detached alpha context · likelihood clipping and logged diagnostics. The archived gamma proximity script targets older output layouts and variable shared-transcript intersections; it is not a ready analysis of this snapshot.

7. A defensible article narrative

The model learns a shared sequence-derived profile that is reproducible across independent dataset collections. Relative to a freshly trained shared-only baseline, the full system improves validation observation fit and reduces discrepancies between shared profiles. The reference-weight policy affects the learned decomposition: linear quality weighting improves several cumulative comparisons, whereas cross-panel benefits are mixed and stronger concentration reduces reproducibility in the available comparisons.

This paragraph describes observations conditional on seed 42. It avoids equating model agreement with biological correctness or calling gamma a verified technical-bias estimate.

Prioritize the next evidence

  1. Run the frozen-checkpoint correct/unit/shuffled-gamma test on genuine held-out observations. This directly tests whether dataset-specific corrections are useful for the dataset they belong to.
  2. Generate common-grid gamma fingerprints with transcript-split stability and metadata controls. Use it as mechanistic support for the functional result, not as a replacement for it.
  3. Add the matched fixed-alpha full/unity comparison and an independent biological endpoint. Together with the existing learned-alpha arms this gives a factorial test. Choose the common fixed alpha using training/validation only; an arbitrary poorly fitted value would weaken the control.

Completing N=80 and N=114 remains necessary for the planned cumulative claim. Additional seeds would assess optimization variability; one seed can support an explicitly scoped pilot, but thousands of test transcripts and six overlapping panel pairs cannot substitute for independent fits. The queueing, local-weight and replicate-likelihood contributions require their own ablations if the article makes separate claims about them.

Current evidence supports a useful full model and a reference-sensitive decomposition. It does not yet support a general superiority claim for quality ranking, a uniquely identified biological Lbio, or gamma as pure technical noise.
