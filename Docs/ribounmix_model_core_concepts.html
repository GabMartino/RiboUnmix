RiboUnmix: the core ideas behind the model

Why Ribo-seq should be treated as a heterogeneous observation process, how the shared profile \(L_t\) and dataset-specific factor \(\gamma_{dt}\) are defined, what the identifiability constraints actually guarantee, and why the joint probabilistic model is more useful than simply taking the geometric mean of all observed datasets.

The central idea of the project. Ribo-seq is not the biological translation process itself; it is an experimental observation of that process. A measured profile contains a mixture of shared sequence-dependent structure, dataset-specific observation effects, and stochastic measurement variability. RiboUnmix is designed to model these components separately rather than asking one neural network to predict the measured profile as if every predictable feature were equally biological.

1. The problem is not just prediction — it is observation

Let \(\mathbf X_t\) be the coding sequence of transcript \(t\), and let \(\mathbf Y^{(r)}_{t,d}\) be the Ribo-seq profile measured in replicate \(r\) of dataset \(d\). Instead of treating the experimental profile as deterministic ground truth, we regard it as one realization of a dataset-specific observation process:

\[ \mathbf Y^{(r)}_{t,d} \sim p(\mathbf Y\mid \mathbf X_t,d), \qquad \mathbf m_d(\mathbf X_t) = \mathbb E[\mathbf Y_{t,d}\mid \mathbf X_t,d]. \]

The expectation is over repeated measurements under the same experimental condition. The difference between a realization and its conditional expectation is

\[ \boldsymbol\varepsilon^{(r)}_{t,d} = \mathbf Y^{(r)}_{t,d} - \mathbf m_d(\mathbf X_t), \qquad \mathbb E[ \boldsymbol\varepsilon^{(r)}_{t,d} \mid \mathbf X_t,d ]=0. \]

The zero mean is not a Gaussian-noise assumption. It follows from the definition of the conditional expectation and is fully compatible with Poisson or negative-binomial count data.

Why reconstruction accuracy alone is ambiguous

Suppose the expected measured profile can be written analytically as

\[ \mathbf m_d(\mathbf X_t) = \mathbf L_t+\mathbf B_{t,d}, \]

where \(\mathbf L_t\) denotes shared sequence-dependent structure and \(\mathbf B_{t,d}\) a systematic dataset-specific component. Under squared error, the Bayes-optimal predictor of the measurement is

\[ f_d^*(X)=\mathbb E[Y\mid X,d]. \]

Therefore a model is rewarded for predicting both \(L\) and any systematic predictable dataset-specific structure \(B_d\). In particular,

\[ \mathcal R_d(f_L)-\mathcal R_d(f_d^*) = \mathbb E[B_d(X)^2\mid d]. \]
Key consequence. A sequence-dependent technical artifact can improve Ribo-seq reconstruction if it is predictable from sequence. Therefore \[ \boxed{\text{reconstruction of measured Ribo-seq}\neq \text{identification of biological translation structure}.} \]

This is the conceptual reason for introducing an explicit observation branch instead of asking one sequence model to absorb everything that helps predict the experimental target.

2. The RiboUnmix factorization

For transcript \(t\), dataset \(d\), codon \(i\), and replicate \(r\), the current model uses a negative-binomial observation model:

\[ Y_{dtri} \mid \mu_{dti},\alpha_{dti} \sim \operatorname{NB2}(\mu_{dti},\alpha_{dti}), \] \[ \mathbb E[Y_{dtri}] = \mu_{dti}, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(Y_{dtri}) = \mu_{dti}+\alpha_{dti}\mu_{dti}^2. \]

The mean is factorized as

\[ \boxed{ \mu_{dti} = S_{dt}\,L_{ti}\,\gamma_{dti}. } \]
\(L_{ti}\)
Shared sequence-dependent positional representation. Dataset identity does not enter the shared encoder.
\(\gamma_{dti}\)
Dataset-specific multiplicative observation effect. It can vary by dataset, transcript, and position.
\(S_{dt}\)
Transcript–dataset scale anchor derived from the observed pair. It removes the need for \(L\) to predict absolute abundance.

The shared branch is normalized to mean one within each transcript:

\[ L_{ti} = \frac{\widetilde L_{ti}} {\frac{1}{L_t}\sum_j\widetilde L_{tj}}, \qquad \frac{1}{L_t}\sum_iL_{ti}=1. \]

This makes \(L_t\) a profile of relative positional occupancy structure, not absolute translation efficiency.

3. Why the current model is mass-free

The scale anchor is

\[ S_{dt} = \frac{1}{L_t} \sum_i \overline Y_{dti}, \]

where \(\overline Y_{dt}\) is the arithmetic replica consensus. The model then uses

\[ \mu_{dti}=S_{dt}L_{ti}\gamma_{dti}. \]

Crucially, the product \(L_t\odot\gamma_{dt}\) is not renormalized. Hence

\[ \frac{\sum_i\mu_{dti}} {\sum_i\overline Y_{dti}} = \frac{1}{L_t} \sum_iL_{ti}\gamma_{dti}, \]

which does not need to equal one.

Why this matters. A sequence-dependent observation effect can genuinely alter the total expected recovered footprint mass. Forcing \(L\gamma\) to renormalize would erase this possibility and make the model treat every observation effect as pure redistribution across positions.

The old mass-conserving alternative would be

\[ \mu^{\mathrm{conserved}}_{dti} = S_{dt} \frac{L_{ti}\gamma_{dti}} {\frac{1}{L_t}\sum_jL_{tj}\gamma_{dtj}}, \]

but this is now best treated as an ablation rather than the default scientific model.

4. Why \(\gamma\) is multiplicative

Many technical steps in Ribo-seq act naturally on relative recovery. Suppose \(\lambda_{ti}\) is the expected underlying footprint abundance and \(q_{dti}>0\) is a sequence- and protocol-dependent relative recovery factor. Then

\[ \mu_{dti} = q_{dti}\lambda_{ti}. \]

This motivates the multiplicative observation model

\[ \gamma_{dti} = e^{g_{dti}}. \]

In log space,

\[ \log\mu_{dti} = \log S_{dt} + \log L_{ti} + g_{dti}. \]

This is consistent with the general structure of log-linear count models, where biological and technical effects are additive on the log mean and therefore multiplicative on the count scale.

\(\gamma\) should be interpreted as a relative observation factor, not as a probability. It may be larger than one.

5. Without a constraint, \(L\) and \(\gamma\) are not identifiable

The unconstrained factorization

\[ \mu_{dti}=S_{dt}L_{ti}\gamma_{dti} \]

has an immediate ambiguity. For any positive position-specific factor \(c_{ti}>0\),

\[ L_{ti}'=L_{ti}c_{ti}, \qquad \gamma_{dti}'=\frac{\gamma_{dti}}{c_{ti}} \]

produces exactly the same product:

\[ L_{ti}'\gamma_{dti}' = L_{ti}\gamma_{dti}. \]

Therefore simply making \(L_t\) dataset-independent is not enough. The model could still move an arbitrary common positional pattern between \(L\) and every \(\gamma_d\).

This is the central identifiability problem: without a gauge convention, “shared” and “dataset-specific” do not have a unique numerical meaning even if the likelihood is perfectly optimized.

6. Fixed-reference centering gives the decomposition a stable meaning

Choose a fixed reference set \(\mathcal R\) of datasets and positive weights \(\pi_d\) with

\[ \sum_{d\in\mathcal R}\pi_d=1. \]

Let

\[ g_{dti}=\log\gamma_{dti}. \]

RiboUnmix imposes two constraints:

\[ \boxed{ \sum_{d\in\mathcal R}\pi_d g_{dti}=0 \quad\forall i, } \] \[ \boxed{ \frac{1}{L_t}\sum_i g_{dti}=0 \quad\forall d. } \]

Cross-dataset constraint

The first implies

\[ \prod_{d\in\mathcal R}\gamma_{dti}^{\pi_d}=1. \]

At each position, the weighted geometric center of the dataset-specific observation effects is therefore one.

Within-profile constraint

The second implies

\[ \left( \prod_i\gamma_{dti} \right)^{1/L_t} =1. \]

This prevents \(\gamma_{dt}\) from becoming an arbitrary transcript–dataset-wide abundance multiplier. In the mass-free model this is an interpretability/model restriction, not an observational symmetry.

The identification theorem

Define the relative expected profile

\[ h_{dti} = \frac{\mu_{dti}}{S_{dt}} = L_{ti}\gamma_{dti}. \]

Under the fixed cross-dataset centering constraint,

\[ \boxed{ L_{ti} = \prod_{d\in\mathcal R} h_{dti}^{\pi_d}, \qquad \gamma_{dti} = \frac{h_{dti}}{L_{ti}}. } \]

Equivalently in log space,

\[ \log L_{ti} = \sum_{d\in\mathcal R} \pi_d\log h_{dti}. \]
What this theorem guarantees. For a fixed reference panel and fixed \(\pi_d\), the decomposition of the model-admitted expected relative profiles into shared positional structure and dataset-specific deviations is unique within the model.
What it does not guarantee. It does not prove that \(L\) is biological ground truth. It is a statement of representational identifiability, not biological identifiability.

7. Why not simply take the geometric mean of all datasets?

This is one of the most important questions for the project because the identification theorem itself contains a geometric mean:

\[ L_{ti} = \prod_d h_{dti}^{\pi_d}. \]

At first sight, this can make the neural model look unnecessary. The correct answer is subtle:

In an idealized noiseless and complete setting, the geometric mean is exactly the right answer. If the true expected relative profiles \(h_{dti}\) were known perfectly for every dataset and every transcript, then under the chosen gauge their weighted geometric mean would indeed equal \(L_{ti}\). RiboUnmix should not claim otherwise.

The real problem is that we do not observe \(h_{dti}\). We observe noisy, finite-read count realizations:

\[ Y_{dtri} \sim \operatorname{NB2}( S_{dt}L_{ti}\gamma_{dti}, \alpha_{dti} ). \]

Therefore a geometric mean of the observed profiles is not the same object as the geometric mean of the latent conditional expectations.

7.1 Geometric mean of counts mixes signal and sampling noise

A direct empirical estimator might look schematically like

\[ \widehat L_{ti}^{\mathrm{GM}} \propto \prod_d \left( \frac{Y_{dti}}{\widehat S_{dt}} \right)^{\pi_d}. \]

But under a nonlinear transformation,

\[ \mathbb E[\log Y] \neq \log\mathbb E[Y]. \]

Thus averaging log observed counts does not generally recover the log expected counts. The discrepancy is especially problematic for sparse and low-depth Ribo-seq profiles.

7.2 Zeros make the naive geometric mean unstable

If any observed count at one position is zero,

\[ \prod_dY_{dti}^{\pi_d}=0. \]

Adding a pseudocount avoids the numerical failure but introduces an arbitrary scale-dependent choice:

\[ \prod_d(Y_{dti}+c)^{\pi_d}. \]

The result can vary substantially with \(c\) in sparse regions.

7.3 Datasets have different uncertainty

A shallow experiment and a deep experiment can measure the same conditional mean with very different reliability. A simple geometric mean operates directly on realized observations and does not explicitly model

\[ \operatorname{Var}(Y) = \mu+\alpha\mu^2. \]

RiboUnmix instead estimates the shared representation through a probabilistic objective over raw replicas and can use transcript–dataset reliability weights during optimization.

7.4 The neural model pools evidence across transcripts

The empirical geometric mean estimates each transcript-position independently from its own observations. RiboUnmix instead learns

\[ L_t=f_\theta(X_t), \]

so transcripts sharing sequence determinants contribute information to the same function. This allows the model to denoise by learning recurrent sequence regularities rather than treating every codon observation as unrelated.

7.5 The geometric mean cannot generalize to unseen transcripts

For a transcript never observed in the reference panel, a geometric mean of experimental profiles does not exist. RiboUnmix can still produce

\[ L_t=f_\theta(X_t) \]

from sequence alone.

7.6 Missing dataset–transcript pairs are natural in the model

Real compendia are rarely complete matrices of all transcripts measured equally well in all datasets. A direct geometric mean either requires restricting to the intersection of observed pairs or inventing a missing-value procedure. The neural model can learn from partial transcript–dataset observations while retaining one shared sequence function.

7.7 Replicates are used correctly rather than averaged away

A geometric mean built from one consensus profile per dataset discards information about within-dataset stochastic variability. RiboUnmix keeps raw replicas as separate draws around the same expected mean.

7.8 \(\gamma\) itself is scientifically valuable

The geometric mean produces only a consensus. RiboUnmix additionally estimates

\[ \gamma_{dti} = \frac{h_{dti}}{L_{ti}}, \]

which provides an interpretable dataset-specific observation profile. This is essential for discovering latent protocol signatures, clustering datasets, testing quality associations, and explaining why experiments differ.

7.9 The model separates estimation from the gauge convention

The geometric-center theorem says how \(L\) is defined once the expected relative profiles \(h_d\) are known. The neural-probabilistic model addresses the harder problem: estimating a family of latent expected profiles from noisy observations under shared sequence constraints.

7.10 A geometric-mean baseline is still important

Because of the theorem, the empirical geometric mean is not a straw-man baseline; it is a scientifically important comparator. A strong paper should compare against at least:

  1. an empirical normalized geometric center of observed profiles;
  2. a two-stage model trained to predict that empirical center from sequence;
  3. the joint RiboUnmix model.

The joint model should demonstrate advantages in held-out synthetic recovery, robustness to depth and sparsity, unseen-transcript generalization, and real-data cross-panel stability.

8. Why raw replicas and the NB2 model matter

For comparable replicas,

\[ Y^{(r)}_{dt} \mid X_t,d \sim p(Y\mid X_t,d). \]

The arithmetic average

\[ \overline Y_{dt} = \frac{1}{R_{dt}} \sum_rY^{(r)}_{dtr} \]

has the same expectation but reduced variance:

\[ \mathbb E[\overline Y_{dt}\mid X_t,d] = m_d(X_t), \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\overline Y_{dt}\mid X_t,d) = \frac{1}{R_{dt}} \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X_t,d). \]

However, averaging replicas first and treating the mean as one observation hides the replicate-level variability. The current training objective therefore retains each raw replicate in the NB2 likelihood while using the arithmetic consensus only for profile-shape terms.

Conceptually: replicates help estimate stochastic uncertainty; multiple datasets help separate shared from dataset-specific systematic structure. These are different sources of information.

9. Why the training objective contains NB2 and PCC terms

For a transcript–dataset pair, the loss has the form

\[ \mathcal L_{dt} = \mathcal L^{\mathrm{NB2}}_{dt} + \lambda_{\mathrm{raw}} \mathcal L^{\mathrm{PCC,raw}}_{dt} + \lambda_{\mathrm{VST}} \mathcal L^{\mathrm{PCC,VST}}_{dt}. \]

NB2 likelihood

The NB2 term models count uncertainty and the empirical mean–variance relationship:

\[ \mathcal L^{\mathrm{NB2}}_{dt} = \frac{1}{R_{dt}} \sum_r \mathcal L^{\mathrm{NB2}} ( Y_{dtr},\mu_{dt},\alpha_{dt} ). \]

Raw PCC

\[ \mathcal L^{\mathrm{PCC,raw}}_{dt} = 1- \operatorname{PCC} ( \mu_{dt},\overline Y_{dt} ). \]

This directly rewards agreement in within-transcript positional shape and preserves the leverage of large peaks and large count differences.

Variance-stabilized PCC

Ribo-seq variance increases strongly with mean. A variance-stabilizing transform for NB2 compresses the high-count dynamic range:

\[ T_\alpha(x) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\alpha+\varepsilon}} \operatorname{asinh} \sqrt{\alpha x+\varepsilon}. \]

The corresponding shape term is

\[ \mathcal L^{\mathrm{PCC,VST}}_{dt} = 1- \operatorname{PCC} ( T_{\alpha_{dt}}(\mu_{dt}), T_{\alpha_{dt}}(\overline Y_{dt}) ). \]

The two PCC terms encourage agreement both on the original count scale and after reducing heteroscedastic dominance of the largest-count positions.

10. Grouped observations and fixed-reference centering solve different problems

These two mechanisms are easy to confuse but have distinct roles.

Grouped transcript observations

When transcript \(t\) is observed in datasets \(A,B,C\), those observations are kept together as one logical training group. The same \(L_t\) must simultaneously explain all views. This provides the cross-dataset evidence needed to learn the decomposition.
Fixed-reference centering

The dataset-conditioned branch is internally evaluated for every reference dataset identity in \(\mathcal R\), even if those rows are not physically present in the current requested batch. This gives \(\gamma\) a stable coordinate system that does not depend on batch composition.
\[ \boxed{ \text{grouping} \rightarrow \text{learning evidence}, \qquad \text{fixed reference} \rightarrow \text{stable mathematical definition}. } \]

The old batch-dependent centering had a conceptual weakness: the value of \(\gamma\) could change depending on which other dataset rows happened to coexist in the batch. Fixed-reference centering removes this dependence.

11. There are three different uses of “weighting”

The project uses several weights, but they should never be conflated.

Quantity Level Purpose Enters training loss?
\(\pi_d\) dataset Defines the fixed-reference gauge / geometric center used for \(\gamma\) No, not directly
\(w_{dt}\) transcript–dataset pair Down/up-weights observations according to empirical reliability Yes
panel stratification variables dataset Construct comparable experimental groups for validation No

Current reliability weighting

Let

\[ D_{dt} = \frac{\sum_i y_{dti}}{n_{dt}}, \qquad C_{dt} = \frac{\#\{i:y_{dti}>0\}}{n_{dt}}. \]

Using a dataset-specific depth reference \(\tau_d\),

\[ q_{dt} = \frac{\sqrt{D_{dt}}} {\sqrt{D_{dt}}+\sqrt{\tau_d}}, \] \[ r_{dt} = 0.70\,q_{dt} + 0.30\,C_{dt}, \]

followed by median normalization within dataset to obtain \(w_{dt}\). The square root makes the contribution of depth sublinear: increasing read depth helps, but doubling an already deep experiment should not double its influence.

Transcript-balanced reduction

The batch loss is reduced first within transcript and then equally across transcripts:

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{batch}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal T_B|} \sum_{t\in\mathcal T_B} \frac{ \sum_{d\in\mathcal D_t^B} w_{dt}\mathcal L_{dt} }{ \sum_{d\in\mathcal D_t^B}w_{dt} }. } \]

Therefore a transcript with many datasets does not automatically receive a larger total vote than a transcript with fewer datasets. The reliability weights redistribute influence within each transcript.

12. What \(L\) and \(\gamma\) can and cannot mean biologically

What \(L\) is by construction

  • sequence dependent;
  • dataset independent;
  • mean one within transcript;
  • the gauge-defined shared component of the expected relative profiles.

What \(L\) is not by construction

  • not guaranteed to be “true biology”;
  • not an absolute translation-efficiency predictor;
  • not protected against a distortion common to every reference dataset.

A common distortion is fundamentally difficult:

\[ h_{dti} = L_{ti}c_{ti}\gamma_{dti}^{\mathrm{unique}} \qquad \text{for every }d. \]

If \(c_{ti}\) is present identically across all reference datasets, the data do not contain cross-dataset information that can identify it as dataset-specific. It becomes part of the shared component.

What \(\gamma\) is by construction

  • dataset-conditioned;
  • position dependent;
  • centered relative to the fixed reference panel;
  • unable to act as a free transcript-wide multiplicative scale because of within-profile centering.

Why \(\gamma\) should not automatically be called “technical bias”

If datasets come from different cell types, perturbations, stresses, developmental states, or genetic backgrounds, a genuine biological context-specific effect may be dataset specific. Therefore the safest general terminology is

\[ \boxed{ \gamma_{dt} = \text{dataset-specific observation effect}. } \]

Technical interpretation becomes strongest in controlled settings where biological material is held constant while protocol changes, or where the learned signature matches a known library-preparation artifact.

13. Why 114 heterogeneous datasets are scientifically valuable

The project becomes especially interesting because protocol metadata are incomplete. The model does not require every nuisance factor to be specified in advance; it can infer dataset-conditioned sequence signatures directly from the profile statistics.

Why heterogeneity helps

Suppose several datasets observe a common signal but with different distortions:

\[ h_{1}=L\gamma_1,\qquad h_{2}=L\gamma_2,\qquad \ldots,\qquad h_D=L\gamma_D. \]

If the \(\gamma_d\) differ across experiments, the model receives multiple views in which different nuisance patterns move while the shared sequence-dependent component remains constrained to be the same.

The useful information is not merely “more data”. It is variation in the observation process around a shared component. This is why experimental diversity can be more informative than simply collecting many nearly identical datasets.

What more datasets cannot solve

If all datasets share the same systematic distortion,

\[ \gamma_{1ti} = \gamma_{2ti} = \cdots = \gamma_{Dti}, \]

then increasing \(D\) mostly reduces stochastic uncertainty. It does not provide the contrast needed to identify that common distortion as dataset specific.

This motivates both the synthetic negative control and the real-data saturation/diversity experiments.

14. Important reviewer questions and precise answers

Question Precise answer
Why is this not just a geometric mean? The geometric-center theorem applies to the latent expected relative profiles \(h_d\), not to finite-read observed counts. RiboUnmix jointly estimates these latent expectations from noisy, overdispersed, incomplete observations, pools information through sequence, generalizes to unseen transcripts, retains replica uncertainty, and additionally estimates the dataset-specific factors \(\gamma_d\).
Does the theorem prove that \(L\) is biological? No. It proves uniqueness of the decomposition within a fixed reference convention. Biological interpretation requires empirical validation on synthetic truth, controlled protocol comparisons, cross-panel reproducibility, and independent biological signatures.
Why not predict only the measured Ribo-seq profile? Because systematic sequence-dependent experimental effects can improve reconstruction. High predictive accuracy therefore does not identify which learned determinants are biological.
Does \(\gamma\) contain only technical bias? Not necessarily. In heterogeneous biological conditions it can contain genuine condition-specific biology as well. It is safest to call it a dataset-specific observation effect unless a controlled design supports a technical interpretation.
Why is \(S_{dt}\) derived from the target? The scientific target of the shared branch is relative positional shape, not absolute transcript abundance. Using \(S_{dt}\) removes an orthogonal abundance-prediction problem and allows the model to focus on within-transcript structure. Comparisons to baselines should use equivalent scale treatment.
Why keep raw replicas? Replicas are separate stochastic realizations of the same expected profile. Retaining them lets the NB2 likelihood learn measurement variability rather than pretending their arithmetic mean is a single exact target.
Why both raw PCC and VST-PCC? Raw PCC emphasizes prominent count-scale features; VST-PCC reduces the dominance of heteroscedastic high-count positions. Together they provide complementary shape supervision alongside the probabilistic likelihood.
Why does fixed-reference centering evaluate datasets not present in the batch? Because the reference panel defines the coordinate system of \(\gamma\). Its value should not change when the same transcript is executed in a different batch. Grouped observed datasets provide learning evidence; the fixed reference provides a stable gauge.

15. Conceptual summary

The project can be summarized as a sequence of increasingly precise claims:

\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c} \text{Ribo-seq is an experimental observation, not unquestioned ground truth} \\[2mm] \Downarrow \\[2mm] \text{predictable technical structure can improve reconstruction} \\[2mm] \Downarrow \\[2mm] \text{model expected profiles as } \mu_{dt}=S_{dt}L_t\gamma_{dt} \\[2mm] \Downarrow \\[2mm] \text{use a fixed reference to make }L\text{ and }\gamma\text{ uniquely defined} \\[2mm] \Downarrow \\[2mm] \text{estimate the latent expected profiles jointly from noisy raw replicas} \\[2mm] \Downarrow \\[2mm] \text{use many heterogeneous datasets to separate stable from moving structure} \\[2mm] \Downarrow \\[2mm] \text{validate }L\text{ by reproducibility, technical depletion, and biological enrichment.} \end{array} } \]

The geometric mean is not the conceptual opponent of RiboUnmix. In the noiseless complete limit, the gauge-fixed shared component is itself a weighted geometric center of the latent relative expected profiles. The contribution of RiboUnmix is to make that decomposition usable in the real setting: noisy count observations, replicate variability, missing dataset–transcript pairs, heterogeneous reliability, sequence generalization, and an explicit dataset-specific observation component whose structure can itself be analyzed.
