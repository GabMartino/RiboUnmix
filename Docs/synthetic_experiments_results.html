Executive answer

The factorization works when the model sees complementary datasets.

More independent bias views consistently improve recovery of the shared biological profile. Gamma recovers the identifiable dataset-specific bias shapes with very high fidelity. Sequencing depth improves reconstruction of observed counts, but depth alone cannot resolve the shared-versus-bias ambiguity of a single dataset.

Within-depth shared recovery, N=2 → N=10 PCC ≈ 0.78 → 0.96

All three read-depth curves improve strongly and almost overlap.

Within-depth identifiable gamma recovery PCC 0.9960–0.9997

Across all 27 cumulative panels; log-gamma RMSE is 0.0089–0.0265.

Quality-rank versus equal reference 8/9 gamma PCC wins

Gamma generally improves; shared Lbio is effectively unchanged.

Single-dataset control μ improves; factorization does not

Mean μ PCC rises from 0.561 to 0.917 with depth, while mean Lbio PCC remains 0.574–0.634.

Central interpretation Predicting a biased profile accurately is not the same as recovering biology. The single-dataset controls can reconstruct their observed target increasingly well as reads increase, yet their gamma is unidentifiable and the learned shared profile remains bias-dependent. The multi-dataset experiments are what identify the decomposition.
Scope of the evidence These are descriptive results from one training seed, one cumulative bias order, the mass-free model, and held-out validation predictions. They establish recovery on the programmed synthetic mechanisms; they do not establish a seed-averaged causal law or guarantee the same decomposition on real data.

02 · Synthetic benchmark

One shared kinetic truth is observed through 30 biased datasets.

Ten systematic RPF-sequence bias mechanisms are crossed with three sampling depths. This creates complementary observations of the same underlying transcript-specific kinetic profile.

Latent biological truth

19,290 deterministic transcript profiles, each normalized to mean one. There is no finite-read sampling in K.

Biased observations

30 bias/depth datasets. Each bias file has 57,870 rows: 19,290 transcripts × two NB2 replicas plus their derived mean.

Held-out evaluation

1,920 validation transcripts per selected run in the current result tables. The latent truth is never used for checkpoint selection.

03 · Model and evaluation

Recovery is evaluated component by component.

The observed-profile fit, shared biological recovery, and dataset-bias recovery answer different questions. Treating μ as the biological output—or comparing raw bias directly with gamma—would give the wrong scientific interpretation.

shape[d,t,i] = gamma[d,t,i] × L_bio[t,i] mu[d,t,i] = S[d,t] × shape[d,t,i] / mean_i(shape[d,t,i])

Lbio: shared shape

Compared with deterministic K after independent mean-one normalization. This is the primary biological-recovery target.

gamma: dataset bias

Compared in log space with the programmed multiplier after applying the same identifiable cross-dataset and positional gauge.

μ: observed reconstruction

Compared with each biased finite-read target. High μ agreement confirms prediction, not necessarily correct factorization.

Why gamma is gauge-fixed

A dataset-constant gamma multiplier disappears when the profile is normalized, and a positional pattern shared across every dataset can be assigned to either gamma or Lbio. The recovery analysis therefore removes both unidentifiable components from learned and programmed log-gamma before comparing them. Gamma is expected to recover the identifiable relative shape, not the raw multiplier b.

Primary protocol Current headline tables use best_val_loss predictions on main_val, score the CDS-observable interior 5 ≤ i < L−5, and compare matched runs only. PCC measures shape; RMSE measures amplitude error. All runs are mass-free and use seed 42.
Two meanings of “quality” Transcript reliability weights balance noisy observations inside the training objective. Dataset quality rank is different: it is used only to choose the fixed gamma-reference gauge in the cross-depth comparison. It is not multiplied into the training loss.

06 · Experiment III

Cross-depth panels separate equal and quality-ranked gamma references.

Each cumulative step adds one bias family at all three depths, so B=1,…,10 corresponds to N=3,6,…,30 training datasets. Every panel is run twice with identical data and seed; only the fixed gamma-reference weighting changes.

Equal reference

Each selected dataset receives the same reference weight. Aggregated across bias families, each depth carries one third of the gauge reference.

Quality-rank reference

Shallow, intermediate, and deep observations receive aggregate reference mass 1/6, 1/3, and 1/2. Training-loss weights are unchanged.

07 · Secondary diagnostics

Interior failures are rare; boundary failures are a representation limit.

Additional analyses test biological structure, large gamma misses, boundary behavior, and possible error absorption into Lbio or alpha. These diagnostics refine the headline recovery result rather than replacing it.

Strong interior gamma sites

The sampled 0.25-depth failure analysis contains 342,732 strongly programmed positions and only 2 strong misses (0.00058%); both occur in the GC-composition family.

CDS boundaries

A detailed within-panel audit reports a 39.65% strong-site miss rate over 10,374 boundary sites. These sites can depend on UTR sequence absent from the CDS-only model and are excluded from primary metrics.

Error compensation

The best-val-loss compensation analysis finds insufficient evidence to attribute residual gamma misses consistently to Lbio leakage or alpha absorption.

Mass conservation

No fair mass-conserved versus mass-free pair is available. Every completed experiment in the current inventory is mass-free, so no mass-mode conclusion is made.

Biological-structure checks

The structural scorecard is a secondary snapshot covering 26 within-depth cumulative conditions (the 20-read/codon N=10 run was not yet present when that report was generated). 08 · Final conclusions

What the synthetic program establishes—and what it does not.

The experiments validate the intended factorization under known synthetic truth. The strongest result is multi-view identifiability: complementary datasets let the model separate shared kinetics from systematic observation bias.

Supported by the results

  • Multiple differently biased datasets recover shared K far better than a single dataset.
  • The shared-profile gain with cumulative panel size repeats at all three read depths.
  • Gauge-identifiable gamma shapes are recovered with near-perfect correlation and small log error.
  • Greater read depth primarily improves μ reconstruction and gamma amplitude precision.
  • Quality-ranked cross-depth centering usually improves gamma while leaving Lbio essentially unchanged.
  • Interior strong gamma misses are extremely rare in the analyzed failure sample.

Not established

  • A pure causal effect of dataset count: panel size and added bias identity are confounded.
  • Training-seed uncertainty: all runs use seed 42.
  • A mass-conservation effect: no matched mass-conserved runs exist.
  • Recovery of raw, ungauged bias magnitude: that quantity is not identifiable.
  • Fair boundary recovery without the missing UTR sequence context.
  • CSS sensitivity in this result snapshot: the exported CSS table is empty.
  • Generalization of synthetic gamma semantics to real biological datasets.

Recommended next experiments

Randomize panel order

Repeat several bias permutations so panel size can be separated from the identity of the newly added mechanism.

Add training seeds

Estimate between-run uncertainty and determine whether the small weighting differences are stable.

Match mass modes

Run identical data panels with and without mass conservation before making a mass-mode claim.

Export held-out test artifacts

Mirror the explicit best-val-loss validation artifact naming on a locked test split.

Supply boundary context

Add the UTR/RPF sequence context needed to evaluate terminal programmed biases fairly.

Pre-register primary metrics

Keep shared PCC/RMSE, gamma PCC/RMSE, and programmed-site tolerance distinct; avoid a composite score.
